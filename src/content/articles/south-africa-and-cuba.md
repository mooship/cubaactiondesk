## The war in Angola

In 1987 and 1988, a major battle took place near the town of Cuito Cuanavale in Angola. [[saho-cuito-cuanavale]]

Angola's army, called FAPLA, had help from Cuba and the Soviet Union. The rebel group UNITA had help from the South African Defence Force (SADF). [[saho-cuito-cuanavale]]

Cuban troops helped defend the town. An early group of 120 Cuban soldiers reached Cuito and helped organise its defences. [[saho-cuito-cuanavale]]

The fighting built up over months. On 14 August 1987, fourteen Angolan and Cuban brigades began a large attack. In October 1987, the Angolan army's 47th Brigade was largely destroyed at the Lomba River. [[saho-cuito-cuanavale]]

The SADF launched its last major attack on Cuito Cuanavale on 23 March 1988. [[saho-cuito-cuanavale]] Keith Somerville, a visiting professor at the University of Kent, calls it the largest conventional battle in Africa since the Second World War. He says it ended with a Cuban bombing of the Calueque dam on 27 June 1988. [[conversation-cuito-cuanavale-2017]]

## Who won?

South African History Online lists three views of the battle: a defeat for the SADF, a tactical withdrawal, or a stalemate. [[saho-cuito-cuanavale]]

The Angolan, Cuban and ANC view is that South Africa wanted to rescue UNITA and take the town's air base. They say Angolan and Cuban forces stopped it. [[saho-cuito-cuanavale]]

Nelson Mandela described the battle as "a turning point for the liberation of our continent and my people." [[saho-cuito-cuanavale]] On a visit to Cuba in 1991, he called it a victory for all Africa. [[conversation-cuito-cuanavale-2017]]

## What came after

Talks followed, and the countries taking part included Angola, Cuba, the Soviet Union, Britain and the United States. [[conversation-cuito-cuanavale-2017]] The talks led to South African and Cuban forces leaving Angola. [[saho-cuito-cuanavale]]

Namibia became independent in March 1990. [[conversation-cuito-cuanavale-2017]] Mandela said the defeat of the SADF made that independence possible. [[conversation-cuito-cuanavale-2017]]

Freedom Park, near Pretoria, names 2,070 Cuban soldiers who died in Angola between 1975 and 1988. [[saho-cuito-cuanavale]]

## Doctors

In 1996, South Africa and Cuba signed an agreement to train South African medical students in Cuba. The first group left in 1997. [[dm-cuba-doctor-programme-2025]]

Students spend one year learning Spanish and five years studying in Cuba. Then they do 18 months of clinical training at a South African medical school. Each bursary student must later work for the state for as many years as the state funded them. [[dm-cuba-doctor-programme-2025]]

In 2018, about 700 fifth-year students came home and none of South Africa's 10 medical schools could take them. [[dm-cuba-doctor-programme-2025]] In 2025, only Gauteng and North West interviewed candidates. [[dm-cuba-doctor-programme-2025]]

Sources disagree on how many doctors the programme has produced. The health department's spokesperson said more than 4,000. [[dm-cuba-doctor-programme-2025]] In November 2022, then Health Minister Joe Phaahla said that 2,617 of 3,369 recruited students had graduated. [[dm-cuba-doctor-programme-2025]] In August 2026, the Cuban ambassador told a parliamentary committee that Cuba had trained 3,243 South Africans as doctors. [[parliament-ir-committee-cuba-statement-2026]]

Cuban doctors have also worked in South Africa. The ambassador said Cuba sent 463 doctors between 1996 and 1998, and 55 of them stayed. [[parliament-ir-committee-cuba-statement-2026]]

In April 2020, 217 Cuban doctors arrived to help South Africa during the Covid-19 outbreak. Health Minister Zweli Mkhize said they were there to help contain the outbreak and were not filling vacancies. [[sanews-cuban-doctors-2020]]

## Engineers

In April 2021, the Department of Water and Sanitation welcomed 24 Cuban engineers. They came to work as advisors in provinces and municipalities. [[gov-za-cuban-water-experts-2021]]

The union Solidarity asked a court to stop the project. The department said it would cost R18.3 million a year, not the R64 million that had been reported. [[mg-cuban-engineers-solidarity-2021]]

The sources we found do not say how the court case ended.

## Cooperation today

In March 2025, South Africa told the United Nations that it had recommitted to cooperation with Cuba in health, basic education, water, sanitation and public works. [[un-sg-report-a-80-83]]

South Africa also wants the US [embargo](/glossary#embargo) on Cuba to end. In August 2026, Parliament's international relations committee backed lifting it immediately and unconditionally. [[parliament-ir-committee-cuba-statement-2026]]

The committee's discussions will feed into South Africa's preparations for the 19th session of the South Africa-Cuba Joint Consultative Mechanism. The committee wants more cooperation on agriculture, renewable energy, tourism, infrastructure and skills development. [[parliament-ir-committee-cuba-statement-2026]]

In September 2026, Minister Ronald Lamola of [DIRCO](/glossary#dirco) repeated South Africa's call for an end to the embargo at the [UN General Assembly](/glossary#un-general-assembly). He used the word "blockade." [[dirco-lamola-unga81-2026]]

For the yearly UN vote, see [How the UN votes on the US embargo of Cuba](/learn/un-votes).
